# Literature review and modeling decisions

Research cutoff: **6 September 2026**. This is a focused technical review of solar-filament segmentation, instance segmentation, thin-structure preservation, and learning with limited/noisy labels. It is not a claim of exhaustive coverage of every paper. Methods and limitations were read in the full texts of MAGFiLO, the 2019 solar U-Net study, Diercke et al., Flat U-Net, EdgeAttNet, and MORDEN. The broader-method papers below were checked through primary publications/author pages; SWEFil was accessible through its publisher abstract, not its full text in the local HTTP client. Local source captures are ignored by Git, with links retained here.

## Main conclusion

The most defensible starting point is a well-validated, high-resolution encoder-decoder with explicit instance reconstruction. There is no paper establishing a universally best architecture for this competition's split, annotators, and PQ evaluator. Model choice must therefore come from our calibration results and a protected holdout. Three issues are more immediately consequential than adding attention: preserving small structures at native resolution; separating instance identity from binary foreground; and distinguishing annotation uncertainty from ordinary background.

The current code implements a compact U-Net baseline and connected components, not every method discussed below. Future methods are labeled as experiments. Public pretrained filament models cannot be assumed safe: many use the full MAGFiLO release, including competition test observations.

## Direct solar-filament evidence

|---|---|---|---|
| **Ahmadzadeh et al., MAGFiLO (Scientific Data, 2024)** | Manual polygon/spine/chirality annotation with independent annotators; dataset collection, review, and selection are documented. JPEG generation already removes large-scale intensity variation. Annotation instructions prefer connected, hole-free masks and omit some faint/small/near-limb structures. | The full public annotation archive overlaps the competition test set. Label absence need not imply physical absence. Annotator records are not independent physical images. | Use only the supplied training JSON; group by physical observation and time; keep separate annotation sets at evaluation. Treat further aggressive flattening as an ablation. [Paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-024-03876-y). |
| **Zhu et al., Solar Filament Recognition Based on Deep Learning (2019)** | Early end-to-end U-Net work establishes that learned segmentation can replace brittle hand-tuned extraction on H-alpha images. | Its task, labels, and pixel metrics differ from the current instance-aware competition. Reported scores do not predict our PQ. | Use U-Net as a measured baseline and retain traditional processing as an interpretable comparator. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.06580). |
| **Diercke et al., universal semi-supervised filament detection (A&A, 2024)** | YOLOv5 boxes on ChroTel support a semi-supervised U-Net pipeline expanded to GONG; transfer to KSO addresses instrument variation. Cross-domain performance is informative but uses different evaluation. Do not import its score as a target or assume its preprocessing fits the sharpened JPEGs. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.07259). |
| **Solomon et al., EdgeAttNet (2025)** | Learned edge priors guide bottleneck self-attention in U-Net; radial flattening, Gaussian smoothing, and CLAHE form the preprocessing. The text's sample-level split does not establish our stricter physical-observation/time grouping. Full-MAGFiLO-trained weights are unsuitable without exclusion proof. | Reimplement edge supervision/attention as an ablation using only our labels. Preserve raw grayscale as a comparison. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.02964), [author code](https://github.com/dasjar/EdgeAttNet). |

## Classical morphology and temporal applications

**Bernasconi, Rust, and Hakim (Solar Physics, 2005)** separate intensity standardization, filament extraction, spine/barb characterization, fragment merging, and tracking. Their method uses directional morphology and seeded region growth after sunspot suppression. Fragment linking uses endpoint proximity and orientation. The inspected method restricts its main characterization to the central disk, and acknowledges confusion between very dark thick filaments and sunspots. These choices suggest useful diagnostic features and a possible orientation-aware grouping ablation, but neither the published thresholds nor the central-disk exclusion should be copied into this dataset without calibration. In particular, joining nearby endpoints can improve continuity while damaging instance identity. Our local-background baseline is a simpler comparator, not a reproduction of their code. [Publisher](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11207-005-2766-y), [research-institute manuscript](https://www.sidc.be/users/evarob/Literature/Papers/Solar%20Physics/2005%20Bernasconi-filament%20detection.pdf).

**Qu et al. (Solar Physics, 2005)** combine contour enhancement, automatic local/global threshold selection, an SVM to distinguish sunspots from filaments, and morphology for spine/footpoint extraction and disappearance tracking. The paper explains why brightness-only spot rejection and a fixed median-based threshold can be unreliable. The transferable lesson is to study the type of false positive rather than simply raising a global threshold. A future candidate could classify already-predicted components using permitted image-derived appearance and shape features, trained only on optimization data; its effect must be evaluated on full PQ, including recall loss. The inspected manuscript and the final institutional citation differ in author listing, so the final publisher record should be used in a formal bibliography. [Author institution](https://digitalcommons.njit.edu/fac_pubs/19704/), [manuscript](https://www.sidc.be/users/evarob/Literature/Papers/Solar%20Physics/2005%20Qu-filament%20detection%20SoPh%20preprint.pdf).

Two related application papers were checked at abstract level: **Riegler et al. (2013)** use intensity-based variational segmentation and postprocessing for filament/flare detection in image sequences; **Chatterjee et al. (2017)** study long-term filament statistics using semi-automated segmentation of Kodaikanal Carrington maps. They concern different outputs and validation settings. They reinforce the importance of morphology, instrument variation, and temporal dependence, but do not establish a score expectation for single-image MAGFiLO instance segmentation. We do not use their data or temporal test-time inputs. [Riegler et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1304.7132), [Chatterjee et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.05658).

## Relevant segmentation architecture families

| Method | Transferable idea | Risk / practical priority |
|---|---|---|
| **U-Net, Ronneberger et al. (MICCAI 2015)** | Skip connections combine context with localization; strong augmentation helps limited annotations. | Binary output needs instance extraction. **Implemented baseline.** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1505.04597). |
| **nnU-Net, Isensee et al.** | Preprocessing, patch geometry, optimization, inference, and data-specific configuration can dominate architecture tweaks. | Primarily medical semantic segmentation; applying the name does not solve filament instances. Adopt the disciplined workflow, not an unverified performance claim. [Primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.10486). |
| **Mask R-CNN, He et al. (ICCV 2017)** | Direct instance proposals with ROIAlign and a per-instance mask head. Boxes can be derived from supplied masks. | Standard low-resolution mask heads may lose thin barbs; long thin objects challenge proposals; overlapping masks require ownership resolution. **Strong instance-aware challenger.** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.06870). |
| **CondInst, Tian et al. (ECCV 2020)** | Instance-conditioned dynamic convolutions remove a fixed ROI mask grid. | More complex training and deployment than our baseline. Promising for thin shapes after baseline failure analysis. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.05664). |
| **Panoptic-DeepLab, Cheng et al. (CVPR 2020)** | Semantic predictions plus center/offset regression produce bottom-up instances. | A single center can poorly represent very elongated, curved objects. Consider boundary/affinity targets before simple centroid offsets. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.10194). |
| **MaX-DeepLab, Wang et al. (CVPR 2021)** | Direct mask-set prediction with a PQ-inspired matching loss. | Data/compute demands and deployment complexity; not the first experiment with 707 images. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.00759). |
| **Mask2Former, Cheng et al. (CVPR 2022)** | Masked attention and set prediction support instance/panoptic/semantic tasks. | Good candidate for direct instance identity; must preserve thin masks through stride/resizing, audit initialization, and enforce non-overlap. **Later high-capacity challenger.** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.01527). |
| **SegFormer, Xie et al. (NeurIPS 2021)** | Hierarchical multiscale features and lightweight decoder provide a useful architecture contrast. | Coarse outputs can lose barbs; semantic output still needs instance reconstruction. Evaluate a small encoder, not the largest by default. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.15203). |
| **Cellpose, Stringer et al. (Nature Methods 2021)** | Learned flows encode object identity for irregular biological shapes. | Solar filaments differ from cell bodies; disconnected appearance and extreme aspect ratio can undermine flow assumptions. Treat as an exploratory representation, not an off-the-shelf solution. [Paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41592-020-01018-x). |
| **SAM / SAM 2** | Prompt-conditioned mask refinement and foundation features may improve local boundaries. | Prompt generation must be fully automatic at test time. Natural-image pretraining, low contrast, elongated fine structures, runtime, and license/provenance need testing. No human test prompts. [SAM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.02643), [SAM 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.00714). |

## Losses, topology, and evaluation

**Panoptic Segmentation, Kirillov et al. (CVPR 2019).** PQ combines matched-mask quality with detection quality: `sum(matched IoU)/(TP + 0.5 FP + 0.5 FN)`. The original non-overlap setting supports unambiguous matches above 0.5. The organizer notebook uses all qualifying pairs and pooled counts over annotator-image entries; reproduce that implementation even where overlapping GT complicates textbook assumptions. A pixel Dice increase can coexist with a PQ decrease. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.00868).

**clDice, Shit et al. (CVPR 2021).** A differentiable skeleton-overlap objective encourages tubular connectivity. It is relevant to barbs and filament continuity, but preserving the topology of a semantic union does not preserve instance identity. Use a small auxiliary weight after foreground learning, derive skeletons only from permitted labels, and inspect new false bridges and merges. Do not turn the paper's theoretical conditions into a blanket guarantee for imperfect solar masks. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.07311).

**Focal loss, Lin et al. (ICCV 2017).** Reduces easy-negative dominance. Filaments occupy little area, so this is plausible; however, hard-example emphasis can amplify annotation disagreement or mislabeled background. Compare against BCE plus Dice while keeping sampling and compute fixed. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.02002).

**Lovász-Softmax, Berman et al. (CVPR 2018).** Provides an IoU-oriented differentiable surrogate. It can help region overlap, but does not directly penalize incorrect instance grouping in the way PQ does. Trial it after a stable instance reconstruction step. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08790).

## Proposed technical pipeline

1. **Freeze inputs and splits.** Hash the competition JSON, image files, decoded pixels, and manifest. Use physical-observation groups, temporal blocks, and embargo. Inspect near-duplicate image similarity before stronger claims; the current audit covers exact duplicates, not every possible near duplicate.
2. **Learn grayscale foreground.** Compare raw JPEG scaling with disk-aware percentile normalization and mild CLAHE as separate experiments. Avoid automatic second limb-darkening removal because the provided JPEG transformation already changes the large-scale profile. Start with one input channel; any added residual/radial channel remains derived from that image alone.
3. **Respect annotator ambiguity.** Current baseline averages each annotator's foreground union into a soft target, with uniform physical-image sampling. Compare random-annotator hard targets and disagreement-weighted losses. Do not pool annotator instance IDs into one target set. Keep the official evaluation unmodified.
4. **Preserve resolution and context.** Compare 1024-pixel full-disk models, native-resolution crops, and a coarse-context/fine-detail cascade. Use overlapping, weighted tile inference when full frames do not fit or crop-dependent normalization causes a domain shift. Restore probabilities before thresholding, using bilinear interpolation; never interpolate categorical instance IDs that way.
5. **Predict instance structure.** Baseline uses connected components with a small optional closing operation. Next compare mask-derived boundary/affinity heads plus seeded reconstruction, and a detector plus a high-resolution refiner. A thin object is not always well represented by a distance-transform center; watershed can over-split branches. Grouping must be supported by image evidence and calibration, not blanket dilation or centroid proximity.
6. **Choose losses by ablation.** BCE plus soft Dice baseline; then boundary loss or small clDice weight. For direct instance models, use assignment/classification/mask losses and study missing annotations. Keep data, split, and step budget fixed when isolating architectural effects.
7. **Calibrate honest decisions.** Select probability, area, and grouping thresholds using calibration PQ plus visual diagnostics. Use native-pixel units. Larger area cutoffs can improve a metric while discarding scientifically interesting small filaments; quantify this cost. Never tune against copied test labels or the public leaderboard.
8. **Ensemble only complementary models.** Select by paired per-image calibration gains and error diversity. Average aligned probabilities for compatible semantic models; reconcile instance sets for direct-instance models. Apply non-overlap after final reconstruction. Measure TTA/ensemble runtime, memory, and benefit.
9. **Run holdout and reproduce.** Once the candidate is frozen, compute pooled PQ, SQ/RQ, grouped confidence intervals, IoU/Dice distributions, split/merge counts, and site/year/size slices. Verify notebook replay and CSV decoding before submission.

## Priority order and stopping criteria

| Priority | Experiment | Promote only if |
|---|---|---|
| P0 | Compact U-Net and classical baseline | Correct evaluator, valid output, reproducible checkpoint |
| P1 | Larger context/capacity; native detail; tiled inference | Calibration PQ gain at matched protocol, acceptable runtime, no visual collapse |
| P1 | Boundary/affinity instance reconstruction | Fewer merges/fragments and a stable gain across sites |
| P2 | Mask R-CNN plus high-resolution refinement | Better recall/instance identity without losing barbs; disjoint output |
| P2 | EdgeAttNet/Flat U-Net/SegFormer ablations | Their claimed advantages reproduce on our split and metric |
| P3 | Mask2Former, self-supervised pretraining | Compute justified by prior experiments; complete provenance |
| P3 | clDice, CRF, model ensemble, TTA | Small isolated improvements survive uncertainty and morphology review |

Published scores above are reported in their original settings. They are **not** a cross-paper leaderboard and are not comparable to this repository's PQ. All candidate gains remain hypotheses until measured. The purpose of the review is to choose useful experiments and avoid known failure modes, not to attach a novel name to an untested combination.
